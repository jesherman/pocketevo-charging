# Incident: 2026-10-02 — "plugged in but drawing nothing"

This documents a full day of Pocket EVO direct-charge failures, the defects found
in this pack, and — importantly — what was *not* established. It is written for
the next person who sees a battery percentage falling while the cable is in.

## What was reported

Two symptoms, several hours apart:

1. Morning: *"seems unstable, 58 > 75 in 2 hours? not right for 28 W"*
2. Afternoon: *"evo is plugged in but not pulling any wattage right now"*

Neither is a charging-speed problem. The 28 W path was working correctly the
whole time.

voltage, input current, adapter power, device power, battery V/I, three
temperatures. UPower keeps rate and SOC history separately. Everything below is

The reported "58 → 75 in 2 hours" is 25 minutes of correct charging followed by
73 minutes of silent drain. The average is meaningless; the *stop* is the bug.

## Defects found in this pack

### 1. The cleanup abandoned the charger at 13 mA

`armada-pocketevo-charge-policy` writes `input_current_limit = 13000` to quiesce
the Qualcomm buck during a direct-charge session. The cleanup restores it — but
its `usb_online = 0` branch sat **above** the restore writes and `exit 0`'d
first. Unplugging mid-session therefore left the stock charger crippled at 13 mA
with nothing to un-cripple it.

Evidence: the 08:47:34 cleanup line reads `both pumps verified off; USB offline`
— the early-exit branch — while the session had been live since 07:09:54.

Fix: the restore always runs. On the offline path the writes are best-effort and
a refusal is not a fault, because there is nothing to charge at that moment.

### 2. Nothing checked whether power was actually moving

Every guard in `direct_session` checks a *state* — pump `online`, `health`,
rail voltage, temperatures, SOC. None checks whether current is flowing. At
07:34:49 the pumps kept reporting `online=1` and `health=Good` with a sane VIN
while delivering exactly zero, so the loop ran for 73 minutes and logged nothing
unusual.

Fix: a `no-delivery` guard ends the session after 60 consecutive samples (30 s)
below 200 mA. It deliberately does **not** latch: the cleanup has already handed
the charger back, so the session ends, the policy backs off 120 s to let the
Qualcomm path take over, and direct charge may start again. Latching here would
turn a recoverable stall into an "unplug the charger" prompt.

### 3. A dead charger could never be repaired

`eligible()` requires `battery/status = Charging`. A charger stuck at 5 V / 0 A
never reports `Charging`, so the policy would sit idle forever next to a device
that needed exactly the write it refused to make.

Fix: `repair_stock_charger()` re-asserts `9600000uV` / `3000000uA` when — and
only when — the USB supply is online, both pumps are off, the battery is
discharging and the charger is drawing under 100 mA. Rate-limited to once a
minute.

### 4. The PPS handshake latched on a transient

The handshake requested 8.8 V, then checked the measured rail once after a 3 s
settle with an upper tolerance of 500 mV. A source still parked at the cleanup's
own 9.6 V park voltage read 9.595 V, tripped the guard, and latched — twice,
costing 18 minutes and two physical cable cycles.

Fix: poll up to 4× with a re-issue, and widen the upper tolerance to 800 mV
while the request stays clamped below the pump's 10.0 V enable ceiling and all
other checks keep their own hard limits.

### 5. Integrator windup drove the rail into the VIN ceiling

Found while verifying the rest, at 15:15. The regulation loop raises the request
whenever combined pump input is under 3.08 A — a figure this adapter in practice
never quite reaches (~3.05 A). Nothing watched the rail itself, so the request
wound up to the 10.5 V clamp and saturated there. Under falling end-of-charge
load the adapter holds its operating point a few hundred mV **above** the
request, the measured VIN crossed the 10.5 V ceiling, and the VIN guard killed a
perfectly healthy session at 88% with a latched "unplug the charger" fault.

This is the same ending the 2026-09-29 session had, also at 88% — a routine
end-of-charge event silently masquerading as a hardware fault.

Fix: regulate on the **measured rail**. Above 10.4 V the request drops 200 mV per
sample; above 10.3 V, 100 mV. This runs before the other regulation terms so it
always wins. The VIN guard becomes a backstop instead of the normal way a
session ends.

## What was NOT established

**The trigger for the 08:47 / 14:41 stuck state is not proven.** Writing 9.6 V to
`voltage_now` recovered a stuck charger, so it was tempting to conclude the
cleanup had left the request low. But writing **5 V** to `voltage_now` while the
charger was live did *not* reproduce the fault — the firmware ignored the write
and kept charging at 1.87 A. Recovery and causation are therefore not symmetric
here.

What *is* established: the stuck state was real (5 V rail, 0 A, battery
discharging, `input_current_limit` readback saying 3 A), and an explicit PPS
request write clears it. Defect 1 is a genuine logic bug regardless, and the
repair function performs exactly the write that recovered the device.

**Defect 2 has not fired in the wild.** It is covered by simulated sessions
(`tests/session-simulation-test.sh`), not by a real stall.

## Unrelated upstream finding

At 06:52:07, on the first cable attach of the day:

```
WARNING: drivers/i2c/i2c-core.h:57 at __i2c_transfer
  __i2c_transfer
  i2c_transfer
  regmap_i2c_write
  nb7vpq904m_set
  nb7vpq904m_sw_set
  typec_switch_set
  typec_set_orientation
  pmic_glink_ucsi_connector_status
  ucsi_handle_connector_change
typec port0-partner: PM: parent port0 should not be sleeping
```

This is armada's `nb7vpq904m` Type-C retimer driver performing an i2c write in
atomic context during resume — the `WARN(!adap->algo->master_xfer_atomic, ...)`
path. It is **not** caused by this pack, but it is worth noting that the retimer
sits on `i2c@988000`, the same bus as the two charge pumps. A misbehaving
transfer there is a plausible route for pump reads to come back wrong, which is
consistent with the 06:52 `measured 9595000uV` readings. Worth reporting to the
armada repo separately.

## Verification

- `tests/charger-restore-test.sh` (28) — cleanup and idle-repair paths against a
  fake power-supply tree.
- `tests/session-simulation-test.sh` (20) — whole sessions driven through the
  policy's own sample hook: the no-delivery guard firing, a 40-sample stall *not*
  firing it, the endpoint guard ending cleanly without latching, and the
  VIN-headroom regulator holding the request under 10.1 V against an adapter that
  runs 400 mV above it.
- `tests/gate-test.sh` (11) — the kernel-identity gate.
- On hardware: policy stop → deliberately set `voltage_now = 5000000` → policy
  start → charging resumed at 29.8 W.
